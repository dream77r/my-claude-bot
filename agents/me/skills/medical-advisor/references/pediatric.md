# Pediatric (0–18 y) Endocrinology & Nutrition Reference

> *Educational reference data — NOT a diagnosis tool. Every threshold, dose, and target below is **general educational information**, not a personal prescription. All pediatric dosing is **weight-based and age-specific**; the **treating clinician** makes the final call on diagnosis, drug, and exact dose given the child's full picture (weight, renal/hepatic function, allergies, comorbidities, other meds, puberty/growth stage, pregnancy in adolescents). Any treatment/dose here must be confirmed with a clinician before use.*

> **⚠️ ОГОВОРКА / CAVEAT (core safety mechanism — must appear on every answer that uses this data):** This is general educational information, NOT a prescription and NOT a diagnosis. **Insulin & insulin titration, levothyroxine (narrow-therapeutic-index), and anything in adolescent pregnancy/lactation are HIGH-RISK — give the educational range but never present a dose as a firm instruction; defer the exact figure to the diabetes/treating team.** Children/teens are assessed by **BMI-for-age PERCENTILES / z-scores, NEVER adult kg/m² cutoffs (25/30)**. Surface RED FLAGS and escalate to urgent/emergency care — this reference never replaces in-person or emergency care. No calorie-shaming of minors.

*Current as of June 2026. Newest editions used: ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes 2026, ISPAD 2024, CDC growth-chart guidance 2025, WHO complementary-feeding 2023, NASEM DRI for Energy 2023.*

## 1. GROWTH / ANTHROPOMETRY

### Which chart, which age (CDC + WHO)
| Age band | Chart to use | Issuing body |
|---|---|---|
| **Birth–24 mo (0–2 y)** | **WHO Child Growth Standards** (prescriptive: how healthy breastfed children *should* grow) | WHO 2006 / CDC adopts for <2 y |
| **2–20 y** | **CDC Growth Reference charts** | CDC 2000 |
| **BMI >97th pct, 2–20 y** | **CDC Extended BMI-for-Age charts** (adds 98th, 99th, 99.9th, 99.99th percentile curves) | CDC 2022 |

CDC formally recommends switching from WHO to CDC charts at **24 months**. (CDC, *Use and Interpretation of WHO and CDC Growth Charts*, 2025 revision)

### Abnormal-growth cutoffs by chart
| Measure | WHO (<2 y) | CDC (2–20 y) |
|---|---|---|
| "Abnormal" SD cutoff | ±2 SD = **2nd & 98th percentile** (2.3 / 97.7) | extreme = <5th or >95th |
| Underweight | low weight/length, z < −2 | BMI-for-age **< 5th percentile** |
| Healthy weight | — | BMI **5th to < 85th percentile** |
| Overweight | weight-for-length > +2 SD | BMI **85th to < 95th percentile** |
| Obesity | weight-for-length > +3 SD | BMI **≥ 95th percentile** |
| Severe obesity | — | **≥120% of the 95th pct OR BMI ≥ 35 kg/m²** (CDC 2022 extended charts) |

> **Emphasis: these are PERCENTILE/z-score cutoffs, NOT adult kg/m² numbers (25/30).** Adult BMI cutoffs do not apply until ~18–20 y.

### Mid-parental (target) height
| Sex | Formula |
|---|---|
| **Boy** | (mother's height + father's height + **13 cm**) ÷ 2 |
| **Girl** | (mother's height + father's height − **13 cm**) ÷ 2 |

Target range = mid-parental height **± ~8.5–10 cm** (≈ ±2 SD; ~9 cm girls, ~10 cm boys covers ~95%).

### Short stature / failure-to-thrive (FTT) — referral triggers (refer to pediatric endocrinology)
- Height **< 3rd percentile** (or < −2 SD), OR height **substantially below** the mid-parental target range.
- **Crossing down ≥2 major percentile lines** on serial measures.
- Growth velocity below normal for age.
- Disproportion, dysmorphism, or signs of systemic/endocrine disease.
- **FTT (infants/toddlers):** weight (or weight-for-length) crossing down ≥2 major percentiles, or weight-for-age < 2nd–5th percentile — evaluate feeding, caloric intake, malabsorption, organic disease.

## 2. PUBERTY — Tanner stages & timing

Tanner / Sexual Maturity Rating: **Stage 1** = prepubertal; **Stage 5** = adult. Staged on breast + pubic hair (girls) and genital + pubic hair (boys). First sign: **breast budding (thelarche)** in girls; **testicular enlargement (≥4 mL / ≥2.5 cm)** in boys.

| Event | Girls | Boys |
|---|---|---|
| Normal onset (Tanner 2) | **8–13 y** (thelarche first) | **9–14 y** (testicular enlargement first) |
| **Precocious puberty** (Tanner 2 onset) | **before age 8** | **before age 9** |
| **Delayed puberty** (no Tanner 2 reached) | **no breast development by age 13** | **no testicular enlargement by age 14** |
| Menarche | typically ~2–2.5 y after thelarche; primary-amenorrhea workup if **no menarche by 15** (or 3 y post-thelarche) | n/a |

(Source: standard Tanner staging; Cleveland Clinic / StatPearls *Tanner Stages*; Endotext/Merck.)

> **Precocious or delayed puberty → refer to pediatric endocrinology** (imaging, bone age, gonadotropins). Note ethnic variation: thelarche somewhat earlier in some populations; isolated early thelarche/adrenarche may be benign but still warrants evaluation if rapidly progressive.

## 3. PEDIATRIC DIABETES

### Diagnostic thresholds (same for T1D & T2D) — ADA 2026
| Test | Diabetes | Prediabetes / at-risk |
|---|---|---|
| Fasting plasma glucose | **≥ 126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L)** | 100–125 mg/dL |
| 2-h OGTT (75 g) | **≥ 200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L)** | 140–199 mg/dL |
| Random PG + symptoms | **≥ 200 mg/dL** | — |
| HbA1c | **≥ 6.5%** | 5.7–6.4% (ADA); HbA1c **5.5–6.4%** flagged as high-risk for youth-onset T2D |

(ADA, *Standards of Care in Diabetes—2026*, Sec. 2)

> **A1c caveat in youth:** HbA1c is **less reliable in children/adolescents** and may under- or over-estimate glycemia (hemoglobinopathy, high RBC turnover, race effects) — **ADA/ISPAD prefer glucose-based criteria (FPG, 2-h OGTT) for diagnosis in youth**; a single abnormal A1c is not enough. Diagnosis (absent unequivocal hyperglycemia) needs **two abnormal results** (same or different tests).

### T1D vs T2D — distinguishing features
| Feature | T1D | T2D (youth-onset) |
|---|---|---|
| Onset | any age, often acute, weeks | peri/post-pubertal, insidious |
| Body habitus | any (often normal/lean) | usually overweight/obese (BMI ≥85th) |
| Autoantibodies (GAD65, IA-2, ZnT8, IAA) | positive | negative |
| Insulin/C-peptide | low | normal/high (insulin resistance), acanthosis nigricans |
| Ketosis/DKA at dx | common | possible (~5–25%) |
| First-line Rx | **insulin (lifelong)** | lifestyle + metformin ± others |

### Glycemic targets
| Target | ISPAD 2024 | ADA 2026 (youth) |
|---|---|---|
| **HbA1c with CGM/AID tech** | **≤ 6.5% (48 mmol/mol)** when safely achievable | individualized; <7% standard |
| **HbA1c standard / limited tech** | **≤ 7.0% (53 mmol/mol)** | **T1D <7%**; **T2D <7%**, <6.5% select pts on lifestyle/metformin without hypos |
| Fasting/preprandial SMBG | 70–144 mg/dL (4.0–8.0 mmol/L) | similar |
| Postprandial SMBG | 70–180 mg/dL (4.0–10 mmol/L) | — |
| Bedtime SMBG | 70–144 mg/dL (4.0–8.0 mmol/L) | — |

### CGM metrics (ISPAD 2024)
| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| **Time in Range (TIR)** 70–180 mg/dL (3.9–10) | **> 70%** |
| Time in **Tight** Range (TITR) 70–144 mg/dL (3.9–8.0) | **> 50%** (>55% if aiming HbA1c ≤6.5%) |
| **Time Below Range (TBR)** <70 mg/dL (<3.9) | **< 4%** |
| Severe low <54 mg/dL (<3.0) | **< 1%** |
| **Time Above Range (TAR)** >180 mg/dL (>10) | **< 25%** |
| Very high >250 mg/dL (>13.9) | **< 5%** |

**Hypoglycemia levels (ADA/ISPAD consensus):** Level 1 = 54–70 mg/dL (3.0–3.9 mmol/L) "alert"; Level 2 = **<54 mg/dL (<3.0)** clinically significant; Level 3 = severe event with altered mental/physical status needing assistance (no glucose threshold).

### DKA diagnostic criteria & severity (ISPAD 2024)
| Criterion | Value |
|---|---|
| Blood glucose | **> 11.1 mmol/L (200 mg/dL)** (may be lower / euglycemic on SGLT2i or in pregnancy) |
| Venous pH | **< 7.3** OR bicarbonate **< 15 mmol/L** |
| Ketones | ketonemia (β-hydroxybutyrate ≥3 mmol/L) and/or ketonuria |

| Severity | pH | Bicarbonate |
|---|---|---|
| **Mild** | < 7.3 | < 15 mmol/L |
| **Moderate** | < 7.2 | < 10 mmol/L |
| **Severe** | < 7.1 | < 5 mmol/L |

> **🚨 DKA is a pediatric EMERGENCY.** Cerebral edema is the leading cause of DKA death in children — avoid overly rapid fluid/osmolar shifts; this is inpatient, specialist-managed care. Escalate immediately.

### Adolescent T2D — screening (ADA 2026)
Screen for prediabetes/T2D **after puberty onset OR age ≥10 y (whichever first)** in youth with **overweight (BMI ≥85th pct) or obesity (≥95th pct)** PLUS ≥1 risk factor:
- family history of T2D
- high-risk race/ethnicity
- signs of insulin resistance (acanthosis nigricans, hypertension, dyslipidemia, PCOS)
- maternal diabetes/GDM during the pregnancy.

Repeat **≥ every 3 years** (or sooner if BMI rising). Test: FPG, 2-h OGTT, or HbA1c.

### TREATMENT & EDUCATIONAL dosing (defer exact dose to clinician)

**T1D — insulin (⚠️ HIGH-RISK, specialist-titrated — never a firm instruction):**
| Phase | Total daily insulin (educational range) |
|---|---|
| ADA initial | **0.5–1.0 units/kg/day** |
| ISPAD prepubertal start | **0.7–1.0 units/kg/day** |
| Partial remission / "honeymoon" | often **< 0.5 units/kg/day** |
| Puberty (insulin resistance) | frequently **> 1.0–1.5 units/kg/day** |

Regimens: basal-bolus MDI (long-acting + rapid-acting analog) or pump/automated insulin delivery (AID); ~40–50% basal / rest as meal boluses by carb ratio + correction factor. **Never self-adjust without the diabetes team.**

**T2D — pediatric, FDA-approved (all ≥10 y unless noted):**
| Drug | Class | Educational dose | Key cautions |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Metformin** | biguanide | start **500 mg PO BID with meals**, ↑ 500 mg/week to **max 2000 mg/day** divided | GI upset; hold for eGFR <30; B12 deficiency risk; lactic acidosis (rare) |
| **Liraglutide** (Victoza) | GLP-1 RA | SC daily, titrated | GI; thyroid C-cell tumor boxed warning (rodent); pancreatitis caution |
| **Exenatide ER** (Bydureon BCise) | GLP-1 RA | SC weekly | same class cautions |
| **Dulaglutide** (Trulicity) | GLP-1 RA | SC weekly | same class cautions |
| **Empagliflozin** (Jardiance) / + metformin (Synjardy) | SGLT2i | PO daily | genital mycotic infections, DKA risk (incl. euglycemic), volume depletion |
| **Dapagliflozin** | SGLT2i | PO daily | same SGLT2i cautions |
| **Insulin** | — | as above | used if HbA1c very high, ketosis, or oral failure |

First-line T2D = **intensive lifestyle + metformin** (± basal insulin if symptomatic/ketotic/HbA1c ≥8.5%); add GLP-1 RA or SGLT2i if targets unmet. (ISPAD 2024 T2D; ADA 2026 Sec. 14)

> **⚠️ CAVEAT (treatment):** The dose figures above are **educational ranges, not a prescription**. Drug choice, exact dose, titration speed, and contraindications (renal/hepatic function, pregnancy/lactation — relevant in adolescents, MTC/MEN2 history for GLP-1 RAs, DKA risk for SGLT2i) are set by the **treating clinician/diabetes team** for that specific child. Pediatric dosing is weight-/age-based — defer the exact figure to the clinician.

## 4. NUTRITION BY AGE BAND

### Energy (Estimated Energy Requirement)
| Age band | Energy (kcal/day) | Notes / source |
|---|---|---|
| 0–<6 mo | ~530–560 | EER, weight-driven |
| 6–11 mo | ~590–750 | rising with weight |
| 12–24 mo | ~830–930 | |
| **2–3 y** | **1000–1400** | by activity |
| **4–8 y** | girls ~1200–1800; boys ~1200–2000 | sedentary→active (Dietary Guidelines / DRI Energy, NASEM 2023) |
| **9–13 y** | girls ~1400–2200; boys ~1600–2600 | |
| **14–18 y** | girls ~1800–2400; boys ~2000–3200 | boys higher; varies by activity |

(Energy cost of growth: boys/girls 4–8 y +15 kcal/d; 9–13 y boys +25, girls +30 kcal/d. NASEM *DRI for Energy*, 2023.)

> *Note: exact per-age-per-activity cells should be confirmed against the NASEM 2023 DRI for Energy tables or the Dietary Guidelines for Americans Appendix 2 before quoting a single figure; ranges above are the widely-consistent published values.*

### Protein (RDA, g/kg/day)
| Age | Protein g/kg/day | g/day approx |
|---|---|---|
| 0–6 mo | 1.52 (AI) | ~9 |
| 7–12 mo | 1.2 | ~11 |
| 1–3 y | **1.05** | ~13 |
| 4–8 y | **0.95** | ~19 |
| 9–13 y | **0.95** | ~34 |
| 14–18 y | **0.85** | boys ~52 / girls ~46 |

(IOM/NASEM DRIs; higher needs in illness/catch-up growth ~1–1.5+ g/kg.)

### Key micronutrients — RDA & deficiency/supplementation
| Nutrient | Pediatric RDA/AI | Supplementation (AAP/standard) | Deficiency note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Vitamin D** | 400 IU/d (<1 y, AI); **600 IU/d (1–18 y, RDA)** | **All breastfed/partially breastfed infants: 400 IU/d from first days of life**; formula-fed taking <32 oz/d fortified: 400 IU/d (AAP) | rickets; common in exclusively breastfed |
| **Iron** | 7–12 mo **11 mg**; 1–3 y 7 mg; 4–8 y 10 mg; 9–13 y 8 mg; **14–18 y boys 11 / girls 15 mg** | Breastfed infants with insufficient complementary iron by 6 mo: **1 mg/kg/day** (AAP). IDA treatment: ~3–6 mg/kg/day elemental iron (clinician-directed) | leading nutritional deficiency; toddlers & menstruating teens at risk |
| **Calcium** | 1–3 y 700 mg; 4–8 y 1000 mg; **9–18 y 1300 mg** | diet first (dairy/fortified) | low intake in teens → bone-accrual risk |
| **Vitamin B12** | 1–3 y 0.9; 4–8 y **1.2 µg**; 9–13 y **1.8 µg**; 14–18 y 2.4 µg | supplement vegan/vegetarian infants & teens; **risk in exclusively breastfed infants of B12-deficient/vegan mothers** | neuro damage if untreated |
| **Iodine** | 1–8 y 90 µg; 9–13 y 120 µg; 14–18 y 150 µg | iodized salt; pregnancy/lactation higher | goiter, cognitive impact |
| **Zinc** | 1–3 y 3 mg; 4–8 y 5 mg; 9–13 y 8 mg; **14–18 y boys 11 / girls 9 mg** | in diarrheal illness (WHO): 10–20 mg/d ×10–14 d | growth faltering, immune |

### Breastfeeding & complementary feeding (WHO 2023 / ESPGHAN)
- **Exclusive breastfeeding to ~6 months**, then introduce complementary foods at **~6 months (180 days)** while continuing breastfeeding to 2 y+ (WHO). ESPGHAN: introduce complementary foods **not before 4 mo, not later than 6 mo**.
- **No added sugar, no sugar-sweetened beverages, low salt** for infants/young children 6–23 mo (WHO).
- Iron-rich complementary foods from 6 mo; avoid honey <12 mo (botulism); avoid cow's milk as the main drink <12 mo.

### Added-sugar limits (AHA)
| Age | Added sugar |
|---|---|
| **< 2 years** | **NONE** (no added sugar) |
| **2–18 years** | **< 25 g (≈6 tsp) per day**; ≤ one 8-oz sugar-sweetened beverage per **week** |

> **Pediatric eating-disorder sensitivity:** present nutrition as *adequacy and balance*, never calorie-shame a minor; use growth-chart tracking, not "dieting."

## 5. PEDIATRIC THYROID

| Condition | Key points | Treatment (educational) |
|---|---|---|
| **Congenital hypothyroidism (CH)** | detected by newborn screen; treat ASAP, ideally **within first 2 weeks**; untreated → irreversible cognitive impairment | **Levothyroxine 10–15 µg/kg/day PO** (often ~37.5–50 µg/day in a term newborn — illustrative); start lower (~25 µg/d) if cardiac risk. Monitor T4/TSH: monthly until normalized, q3mo to age 3 y, then q6mo |
| **Acquired hypothyroidism** (Hashimoto most common) | fatigue, poor growth, delayed puberty, goiter, ↑TSH | levothyroxine, weight-based (older children ~2–5 µg/kg/d, decreasing with age toward adult ~1.6 µg/kg/d), titrate to TSH |
| **Hyperthyroidism / Graves** | tachycardia, weight loss, tremor, goiter, exophthalmos | methimazole first-line (PTU avoided in children except early pregnancy/thyroid storm due to hepatotoxicity); specialist-managed |

> ⚠️ Levothyroxine is a **narrow-therapeutic-index** drug — exact dose, brand consistency, and lab-guided titration are clinician-set. Over-treatment in infants risks craniosynostosis; under-treatment risks neurodevelopment. Never present a firm dose; defer to the treating clinician.

## 6. EATING DISORDERS & GROWTH-FALTERING RED FLAGS

- **No low-BMI cutoff is required for diagnosis** — eating disorders occur at *any* weight/BMI percentile (incl. "atypical anorexia" in normal/high-weight youth). Watch the **trajectory** (crossing down percentiles), not just absolute weight.
- Suspect with: unexplained weight loss **or failure to gain expected weight**, **crossing down growth percentiles**, **unexplained growth/pubertal delay or primary/secondary amenorrhea**, restrictive/abnormal eating, over-exercise, recurrent vomiting, preoccupation with food/body/exercise.
- **Bradycardia is often the first/most common physiologic sign**; also orthostasis, hypothermia, syncope, electrolyte abnormalities → ECG and labs.

### Medical-instability / admission red flags (SAHM criteria)
| Red flag | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Bradycardia | **HR < 50 bpm daytime** (< 45 at night) |
| Hypotension | **BP < 90/45 mmHg** |
| Hypothermia | **< 35.6 °C (96 °F)** |
| Orthostatic changes | significant HR/BP drop on standing |
| Marked underweight | **< 75% of median/ideal body weight for age & sex** |
| Electrolytes | hypokalemia, hypophosphatemia (refeeding risk), hypoglycemia |

> Any of these → urgent medical evaluation / likely inpatient admission. Refeeding must be medically supervised (refeeding-syndrome risk).

## 7. PEDIATRIC RED-FLAG SYMPTOMS — URGENT / EMERGENCY

Core endocrine/nutrition emergencies (always surface and escalate to urgent/emergency care):
- **New diabetes with DKA signs** (polyuria/polydipsia + vomiting, deep/rapid breathing, fruity breath, drowsiness, abdominal pain).
- **Severe hypoglycemia** (seizure, unconsciousness, inability to self-treat — Level 3).
- **Eating-disorder medical instability** (bradycardia, hypotension, hypothermia, syncope, electrolyte derangement per SAHM table above).
- **Any infant/child with dehydration, lethargy, or poor perfusion.**
- **Adrenal crisis** in a child on/with insufficient steroid cover (hypotension, vomiting, hypoglycemia, altered consciousness).

When in doubt about an acutely unwell child → urgent in-person / emergency care, not chat.

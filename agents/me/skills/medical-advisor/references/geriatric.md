# Geriatric (65+) Endocrinology & Nutrition — Reference Data

> **Educational reference data — not a diagnosis tool.** This file is on-demand reference material for an older-adult (65+) family-health advisor. Any treatment or dose below is **educational** and **must be confirmed with the treating clinician/pharmacist** before use. The physician makes the final call on diagnosis, drug choice, and exact dose given the person's full clinical picture — comorbidities, renal/hepatic function, current meds and interactions, allergies, frailty, cognition, falls, and life expectancy.

> **Оговорка / Educational caveat (must accompany every use):** In older adults the burden/harm of treatment frequently outweighs the benefit, so **de-intensification and individualized (relaxed) targets are the default, not the exception.** High-risk items — **insulin & titration, anticoagulants, sulfonylureas, levothyroxine and other narrow-therapeutic-index drugs** — carry **extra "confirm with your doctor/pharmacist first"** framing and the exact figure is always deferred to the clinician. Red flags (Section 5) are escalated to urgent/emergency care and are never managed at home.

Newest editions reflected as of June 2026: ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes **2026** (publ. Dec 2025); AGS **Beers 2023**; EWGSOP2 (revised European consensus, current); PROT-AGE 2013; ESPEN geriatrics 2022; Endocrine Society vitamin D 2024.

## 1. GERIATRIC DIABETES — targets relax with frailty

*(ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes **2026**, Section 13 "Older Adults")*

**Why targets relax.** Older adults with shorter life expectancy, advanced complications, life-limiting comorbidities, frailty, or substantial cognitive/functional impairment derive **less benefit** from tight glucose-lowering and are **more likely to be harmed** (hypoglycemia → falls, fractures, arrhythmia, sudden death, accelerated cognitive decline). Statins and intensive control need a multi-year benefit window (statins ≈ 2.5 yrs) that may exceed life expectancy. But severe hyperglycemia (**>180 mg/dL / >10 mmol/L**) is still avoided — it drives dehydration, weakness, infection, poor wound healing, and HHS.

### HbA1c & glucose targets stratified by health status

*(ADA 2026, Table 13.2 / accompanying figure)*

| Health status (ADA category) | Reasonable **HbA1c** | Fasting / preprandial glucose | Bedtime glucose |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Healthy** — few coexisting chronic illnesses, intact cognition & function | **<7.0–7.5%** (<53–58 mmol/mol) | 80–130 mg/dL (4.4–7.2 mmol/L) | 80–180 mg/dL (4.4–10.0 mmol/L) |
| **Complex / intermediate** — ≥3 chronic illnesses and/or ≥2 IADL impairments, mild–moderate cognitive impairment | **<8.0%** (<64 mmol/mol) | 90–150 mg/dL (5.0–8.3 mmol/L) | 100–180 mg/dL (5.6–10.0 mmol/L) |
| **Very complex / poor health** — LTC, end-stage chronic illness, moderate–severe cognitive impairment, ≥2 ADL dependencies | **Avoid reliance on HbA1c**; goal = avoid hypoglycemia & symptomatic hyperglycemia | 100–180 mg/dL (5.6–10.0 mmol/L) | 110–200 mg/dL (6.1–11.1 mmol/L) |

**CGM time-in-range (ADA 2026, older adults):**
- **Healthy** → TIR (70–180 mg/dL) **≥70%**; time-below-range (TBR) <70 mg/dL **≤4%** (and <54 mg/dL **<1%**).
- **Complex / intermediate** → TIR **≥50%**; TBR <70 mg/dL **<1%**.
- **Very complex / poor health** → no separate CGM number is specified; the focus shifts away from metrics toward **avoiding hypoglycemia and symptomatic hyperglycemia**.
- *In older/high-risk adults, minimizing TBR (hypoglycemia) takes priority over maximizing TIR.*

**BP & lipids (ADA 2026, older adults):**
- BP **<130/80 mmHg** for healthy & complex/intermediate **if achievable without treatment burden, orthostasis, or falls**; relax to **<140/90 mmHg** for very-complex/poor health.
- Statin therapy for most, unless limited life expectancy or intolerance.

### De-intensification / deprescribing (ADA 2026 — explicit recommendations)
- **Deintensify** hypoglycemia-causing agents (insulin, sulfonylureas, meglitinides) in those at high hypoglycemia risk; deintensification is shown **safe and possibly beneficial**.
- **Simplify** complex insulin regimens (multiple daily injections → once-daily basal where possible) to cut hypoglycemia and polypharmacy.
- Deintensify when **harms/burdens > benefits**; align goals with life expectancy and patient/caregiver priorities.

### Drug classes — caution / avoid in older adults

*(ADA 2026 + AGS **Beers 2023**)*

| Class | Geriatric caution (educational) | Renal / age dosing notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Metformin** — preferred first-line if tolerated | Safe to eGFR ≥30; GI intolerance, B12 depletion | **Do not initiate** if eGFR **<45**; if already on it and eGFR falls to **30–45**, reassess risk/benefit and **consider ~50% dose reduction**; **stop if eGFR <30**. Check **B12 periodically** with long-term use (ADA/Beers flag metformin-associated B12 deficiency; no single fixed interval mandated — commonly annually after several years of use). Monitor renal function q3–6 mo. |
| **Sulfonylureas** ⚠️ | **Beers 2023: AVOID** as a class in older adults — prolonged hypoglycemia, ↑CV risk, falls/fracture. **Glyburide (glibenclamide) and chlorpropamide are the most strongly avoided** (long-acting, highest hypoglycemia). If a sulfonylurea is unavoidable, the **shorter-acting, hepatically-metabolized glipizide at the lowest dose** is the least-bad option (glimepiride still renally influenced). | Renally cleared/long-acting agents accumulate → severe, prolonged hypoglycemia |
| **Meglitinides** | ↑hypoglycemia & fracture risk | — |
| **Insulin** ⚠️ **HIGH-RISK — defer exact dose / titration to clinician** | **Beers: avoid sliding-scale insulin** for chronic management. Prefer **once-daily basal (glargine/detemir/degludec)**; simplify regimens; titrate slowly | Renal impairment prolongs action → hypoglycemia |
| **Pioglitazone (TZD)** | Caution: HF, fluid retention, weight gain, ↑fracture/osteoporosis, macular edema | Avoid in HF |
| **GLP-1 RA** — preferred for ASCVD / CKD / obesity | GI side effects → weight-loss / sarcopenia risk in frail; nausea, volume depletion | Most usable to low eGFR (agent-specific) |
| **SGLT2 inhibitor** — preferred for HF / CKD / ASCVD | Volume depletion / orthostasis (esp. on diuretics), genital infection, **euglycemic DKA (risk ↑ with age)**; correct volume before starting | **Initiate down to eGFR ~20**; glucose-lowering effect weaker at eGFR 45–60 (cardiorenal benefit persists) |
| **DPP-4 inhibitor** | Weight-neutral, low hypoglycemia → useful in frail | Dose-adjust for renal (except linagliptin) |

> *Note on CKD thresholds:* the metformin eGFR cutoffs (don't initiate <45, stop <30) and SGLT2i initiation to eGFR ~20 align with ADA 2026 and product labeling. KDIGO diabetes-in-CKD specifics should be verified against the KDIGO source if cited directly.

## 2. SARCOPENIA & FRAILTY

### Sarcopenia — EWGSOP2 (revised European consensus; current operative definition)

**Algorithm: Find → Assess → Confirm → Severity.**

- **Find (screen):** **SARC-F** questionnaire — 5 items (Strength, Assistance walking, Rise from chair, Climb stairs, Falls), each 0–2, total 0–10. **Score ≥4 = positive → assess further.**
- **Assess (strength = primary):** **Grip strength** low if **<27 kg men / <16 kg women**; or **chair-stand >15 s** for 5 rises. Low strength = **"probable sarcopenia."**
- **Confirm (muscle quantity):** DXA appendicular lean mass — **ASM <20 kg men / <15 kg women** (or ASM/height² **<7.0 men / <5.5 women kg/m²**); BIA acceptable.
- **Severity (physical performance):** **gait speed ≤0.8 m/s**; or SPPB ≤8; TUG ≥20 s; 400-m walk fail.

### Frailty — Fried physical phenotype (5 criteria)

Unintentional **weight loss >10 lb (≥4.5 kg) or ≥5%/yr**; self-reported **exhaustion**; **weakness** (low grip); **slow gait**; **low physical activity**. **0 = robust, 1–2 = pre-frail, ≥3 = frail.** (Clinical Frailty Scale 1–9 is the common bedside alternative.)

### Protein needs — higher than in younger adults

*(PROT-AGE 2013 + ESPEN geriatrics 2022; both remain the operative references as of June 2026)*

| Population | Protein target | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Healthy older adult | **1.0–1.2 g/kg/day** (ESPEN: **≥1.0**) | PROT-AGE / ESPEN |
| Acute/chronic illness, malnourished / at risk | **1.2–1.5 g/kg/day** | PROT-AGE |
| Severe illness / injury / major undernutrition | **up to 2.0 g/kg/day** | PROT-AGE |
| Severe renal disease (eGFR <30) **not** on dialysis | cap ~**0.8 g/kg/day** (exception — individualize) | PROT-AGE |
| Per-meal distribution to trigger muscle protein synthesis | ~**25–30 g** protein/meal (~2.5–2.8 g leucine) | PROT-AGE |

**Resistance exercise is the cornerstone.** Progressive resistance training **~2–3×/week** + adequate protein is the most evidence-based intervention to build/preserve muscle; combine with aerobic + balance training.

**Unintentional weight loss to work up:** **≥5% body weight over 6–12 months** (or >10 lb / 4.5 kg/yr) is a recognized threshold → evaluate (malignancy, depression, dysphagia, malabsorption, hyperthyroidism, meds, dental/social/financial causes).

## 3. GERIATRIC NUTRITION / MALNUTRITION

**Screening / diagnosis:**
- **MNA** (Mini Nutritional Assessment) — **24–30 = normal; 17–23.5 = at risk; <17 = malnourished**.
- **GLIM** 2-step (screen → phenotypic [weight loss / low BMI / low muscle mass] **+** etiologic [reduced intake/absorption or inflammation]) is the converging global standard. MNA tends to over-diagnose, so **pair with GLIM**.

**ESPEN geriatrics 2022 — energy / protein / fluid** *(approximate; individualize):*
- **Energy ~30 kcal/kg/day** (REE ≈20 kcal/kg, total range 24–36).
- **Protein ≥1.0 g/kg/day** (see §2 for illness/malnutrition uplift).
- **Oral nutritional supplements (ONS)** for malnutrition/risk: **≥400 kcal/day incl. ≥30 g protein/day**.
- **Hydration:** all older adults are at risk of low-intake dehydration. EFSA adequate intake **~2.0 L/day women, ~2.5 L/day men** (drinks + food); a common clinical floor is **~1.6 L/day women / 2.0 L/day men** from drinks. *Note: exact figures vary by source (EFSA adequate-intake vs clinical floor) — treat as approximate.* Lower thirst sensation + diuretics/SGLT2i raise risk.

**Common deficiencies & educational repletion ranges** ⚠️ *(confirm dose, route, duration, and lab recheck with clinician/pharmacist — these are widely-used educational ranges from drug references, not a single 2026 guideline regimen):*

| Nutrient | Why common in elderly | Educational repletion |
|---|---|---|
| **Vitamin D** (common in elderly; prevalence varies widely by population and cutoff) | Less skin synthesis, less sun, low intake | **Maintenance ~800–1000 IU/day** (IOF, bone health). **Endocrine Society 2024: empiric daily vitamin D for adults ≥75** (low daily dose preferred over high intermittent bolus dosing). **Treating documented deficiency (25(OH)D <20 ng/mL / <50 nmol/L):** a commonly-used drug-reference regimen is **~50,000 IU weekly ×6–8 wk**, then maintenance — confirm regimen/route with clinician. A frequently-cited sufficiency/bone target is 25(OH)D **≥30 ng/mL (75 nmol/L)** (note: Endocrine Society 2024 stepped back from universal screening/aggressive targets — individualize). |
| **Vitamin B12** (common; metformin / PPI / atrophic gastritis) | Malabsorption | **Oral 1000–2000 µg/day** (as effective as IM for most, including many with pernicious anemia once stable). **IM** for severe / neurologic / acute pernicious presentations: e.g. **1000 µg several times in the first 1–2 wk → weekly → monthly maintenance** (regimens vary — clinician-directed). |
| **Calcium** | Low dairy intake, ↓absorption | **1000–1200 mg/day total (diet + supplement)**; women ≥51 and men ≥71 → **1200 mg/day**. Prefer dietary; split doses to ≤500–600 mg. |
| **Protein** | Anabolic resistance, poor intake | see §2 |
| **Folate, iron, magnesium, zinc** | Intake / absorption | individualize; treat documented deficiency |
| **Water** | Blunted thirst | see hydration above |

**Appetite / dysphagia / oral health:** screen for dysphagia (texture-modified diets, IDDSI levels, SLT referral, aspiration risk), poor dentition, depression, polypharmacy-induced anorexia/dysgeusia. **Avoid restrictive/therapeutic diets** (low-salt, low-fat, "diabetic") in frail elderly when they worsen intake/weight loss — **liberalize the diet.**

**Bone health / osteoporosis:** calcium + vitamin D as above; weight-bearing/resistance exercise; fall prevention; screen with DXA (**T-score ≤−2.5 = osteoporosis**). Pharmacotherapy (bisphosphonates, denosumab, anabolics) is clinician-directed. ⚠️ Watch calcium–levothyroxine and calcium–iron–PPI **absorption interactions** (separate dosing by ≥4 h).

**Polypharmacy–nutrition interactions (examples):** metformin ↓B12; PPIs ↓B12/Ca/Mg; loop diuretics ↓K/Mg/thiamine + dehydration; levothyroxine absorption blocked by Ca/Fe/PPI/fiber; **warfarin × vitamin K intake — keep intake consistent, not zero** ⚠️ high-risk anticoagulant; any diet change should be cleared with the anticoagulation clinic.

## 4. THYROID IN THE ELDERLY

- **Age-shifted TSH:** the upper reference limit **rises with age** — mild TSH elevation can be physiologic in older adults. French Endocrine Society rule of thumb: **upper TSH limit ≈ age/10 mIU/L** when screening/following elderly (a rule of thumb, not a universal lab cutoff); many people ≥70–80 sit normally up to ~6–7 mIU/L.
- **Subclinical hypothyroidism (normal FT4, high TSH):**
  - **TSH 4.5–6.9 mIU/L** → generally **do not treat** (low progression risk; relaxed elderly targets).
  - **TSH 7–9.9 mIU/L** → **patient-tailored** decision (weigh cardiac & bone risk, symptoms).
  - **TSH ≥10 mIU/L** → treatment usually warranted.
  - **Age ≥80–85 with TSH ≤10** → **avoid levothyroxine where possible** (TRUST / IEMO trials showed no symptom/QoL benefit; overtreatment → AF, fracture).
- **Levothyroxine starting dose in the elderly** ⚠️ *(narrow-therapeutic-index; defer titration to clinician):*
  - Full-replacement estimate (ATA) ≈ **1.6 µg/kg/day** in healthy adults, **but in elderly / cardiac disease start LOW: 12.5–25 µg/day** (absolute dose), titrate **12.5–25 µg every 4–6 weeks** (some use 2–3 wk), guided by TSH, targeting **TSH in the upper-normal / age-adjusted range** (avoid over-suppression → AF, osteoporosis, fracture).
  - Take on an empty stomach; separate from calcium / iron / PPI by ≥4 h.

## 5. GERIATRIC RED FLAGS — urgent / emergency

**Always surface and escalate. Never manage at home.**

| Red flag | Concern |
|---|---|
| **Severe hypoglycemia** — confusion, seizure, unconsciousness, or needing another person's help; recurrent lows or **hypoglycemia unawareness** | Insulin/sulfonylurea emergency → falls, arrhythmia, sudden death; **call emergency services** |
| **Hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state (HHS)** — glucose often **≥600 mg/dL**, profound dehydration, confusion, slurred speech, focal weakness, lethargy/coma | Life-threatening diabetic emergency of older T2D → **ER now** |
| **DKA / euglycemic DKA on SGLT2i** — nausea/vomiting, abdominal pain, rapid breathing, ketones (even with near-normal glucose) | Emergency |
| **Acute stroke signs** (FAST), new focal deficit, sudden severe headache | Emergency |
| **Falls with injury / syncope / suspected hip fracture** | Urgent — often hypoglycemia / orthostasis / over-treatment driven |
| **Rapid unintentional weight loss / new dysphagia, choking, aspiration, food "sticking"** | Malignancy, severe malnutrition, unsafe swallow → urgent workup |
| **Signs of severe dehydration** — minimal urine, dizziness / orthostatic collapse, acute confusion (delirium) | Urgent, esp. on diuretics/SGLT2i |
| **New / worsening confusion or delirium** | Often infection, drug, metabolic (glucose/Na), hypoglycemia → urgent eval |
| **Thyroid storm / myxedema coma** — extreme agitation + fever, OR hypothermia + marked lethargy/unresponsiveness | Endocrine emergency |
| **Severe hyponatremia symptoms** (confusion, seizure) — SIADH / thiazides / SSRIs common in elderly | Emergency |
| **Anticoagulant + fall / head injury, or any major bleeding** ⚠️ | Emergency — intracranial bleed risk |

### Source notes & recency flags (June 2026)
- **ADA 2026 Section 13** numeric targets corroborate the 2025 edition and appear unchanged in substance; exact Table 13.2 cell wording should be verified against the official *Diabetes Care* PDF before being quoted verbatim. CGM target for the very-complex group is intentionally non-numeric (focus on avoiding hypo/symptomatic hyper).
- **ESPEN geriatrics 2022** remains current (no 2025/2026 replacement confirmed); energy 30 kcal/kg, protein ≥1.0 g/kg, ONS ≥400 kcal/≥30 g are consistent across sources. Hydration mL/kg figures vary by source — flagged approximate.
- **PROT-AGE (2013)** and **EWGSOP2** remain operative; no superseding 2025/2026 consensus confirmed.
- **Levothyroxine elderly start 12.5–25 µg/day** is the absolute dose (a per-kg reading would be a gross overdose); ATA full-replacement **1.6 µg/kg/day** confirmed. "TSH upper limit = age/10" is a French Endocrine Society rule of thumb.
- **Vitamin D / B12 repletion regimens** are widely-used educational drug-reference ranges, not a single 2026 guideline; exact regimen/route is clinician-determined. **Endocrine Society 2024** vitamin D guideline (empiric daily supplementation for adults ≥75; argues *against* routine 25(OH)D screening in healthy adults and *against* high-dose intermittent bolus dosing) is the newest dedicated guideline and is confirmed. Specific deficiency-prevalence percentages were removed from §3 because they vary too much by population/assay/cutoff to state as fact.
- **AGS Beers 2023** is the current Beers edition (no 2025 update confirmed); "avoid all sulfonylureas" and "avoid sliding-scale insulin" are confirmed.
- **KDIGO** diabetes-in-CKD numeric thresholds were not independently fetched beyond what aligns with ADA 2026 / labeling; verify if cited directly.
